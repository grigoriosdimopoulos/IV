How to talk like a Senior Architect (not a senior dev)

Frame every answer around trade-offs. "It depends on X" is not a cop-out at this level — it's the answer. Architects don't pick technologies, they justify them.

  • Always lead with the requirement, then the constraint, then the choice. Not "we used Kafka", but "throughput was 50k/s with replay needs → Kafka over RabbitMQ".
  • Reference real numbers from past work (Kaizen Gaming, Tymit, Camelot, betting/fintech volumes). Vague claims kill credibility.
  • Show you think about Day 2: observability, on-call, rollback, blast radius, cost. Architects own systems after launch.
  • When asked "how would you design X": clarify non-functional reqs first (RPS, latency SLO, consistency model, data volume). Never start drawing boxes immediately.
  • If you don't know something (TOGAF, OpenShift specifics, Wicket): say so, then bridge to what you do know. Hiding gaps gets caught; bridging shows judgment.
  • This is SYNTAX consulting — finance/insurance/public sector clients. Emphasize regulated environment thinking: auditability, GDPR, change management, vendor lock-in considerations.
  • Insurance/finance regulators care about: data lineage, audit trails, right-to-erasure (GDPR), retention (7-10 years), encryption at rest + in transit, segregation of duties, change advisory board.
  • Don't oversell microservices. SYNTAX clients run mainframes. Modular monolith + strategic extractions is more honest than "rewrite everything as microservices".
